# Script Development

## Source fidelity

Use the selected complete article as the primary source of truth. Internally identify:

1. main argument;
2. strongest tension or contradiction;
3. counterintuitive point;
4. practical workplace problem;
5. strongest example, observation, framework, or data point;
6. why it matters to the target business audience.

Never invent statistics, research findings, company examples, quotations, causal claims, or expert opinions. Separate article facts from interpretation and adapt only claims the source can support.

## Angle selection

Create exactly three genuinely different editorial angles. They must not be paraphrases of the same argument. Possible framing types include Contrarian, Problem / Consequence, and Reframe / Insight; do not mechanically force these categories if another set better fits the source.

## Length, hook, and retention

Each script must be approximately 100–130 spoken words, approximately 45–60 seconds, and make one strong argument rather than many shallow points.

The first five seconds must create at least one of: tension, contradiction, curiosity, recognizable workplace problem, challenge to a common belief, or unexpected consequence. Avoid generic openings such as:

- “In today's fast-changing world...”
- “Leadership is more important than ever...”
- “Communication is essential...”
- “Have you ever wondered...”
- “Here are three tips...”

Include at least one meaningful mid-video retention beat, normally around the middle: a surprising consequence, reversal, concrete example, contrast, question, short punch line, evidence, or shift from assumption to reality. Do not write a strong hook followed by 40 seconds of flat explanation and then an ending.

## Writing style

Write for speech, not reading. Use natural spoken English, short sentences, clear transitions, simple vocabulary, varied sentence length, strong contrast, concrete language, one clear argument, and a professional but conversational tone.

Avoid academic wording, corporate jargon, excessive buzzwords, motivational clichés, vague management advice, obvious AI phrasing, overly dramatic clickbait, and cramming the full source article into one video.

## Style reference only

Use the following transcript only as a reference for spoken rhythm, sentence length, clarity, pacing, information density, contrast, and a strong closing reframe. Do not copy its exact structure. Avoid repeatedly generating “For years...”, “But something changed...”, or “That's why...”.

```text
For years, HR was measured by one thing:

How fast you could hire.

The faster you filled a role, the better you were.

But something changed.

Not because HR became less important,

but because business did.

Today, finding people isn't the hardest part anymore.

Finding the right people,

in the right country,

while staying compliant,

and keeping teams working across borders.

The best HR professionals aren't just recruiters anymore.

They're talent strategists.

They don't just fill positions.

They help companies grow,

and they help people build better careers.

That's why global hiring is no longer just about recruitment.

It's about building the right workforce.
```
